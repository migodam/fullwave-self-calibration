## 0. Shared foundations every experiment must implement

### 0.1 Non-negotiable semantic separation (from §1, §2.1)

Three different “ranks” must never be conflated:

$$L_{\mathrm{det}}:\ \text{number of data-determined current components},\qquad
r_{\mathrm{num}}:\ \text{numerical state-approximation dimension},\qquad
r_{\mathrm{free}}:\ \text{extra free-current nuisance dimension}.$$

The monotone-information-erosion statements apply to the third quantity
($r_{\mathrm{free}}$), not automatically to the first two. Increasing the original SOM
cutoff $L_{\mathrm{det}}$ shrinks the complementary ambiguous space; solving the same
physical state more accurately does not reduce the Fisher information of that physical
statistical model.

### 0.3 The two legal parameterizations (from §2.2)

Let

$$G_{\chi,\ell}=D_{E_{\mathrm{tot},\ell}}T_\ell.$$

**Independent-current form** (direct map derivative is zero):

$$\begin{aligned}
\delta y_\ell
&=S_\ell\delta j_\ell+H_{S,\ell}h,\\
M_\ell\delta j_\ell-G_{\chi,\ell}\delta\chi-H_{D,\ell}h&=0.
\end{aligned}\tag{2.1}$$

If $M_\ell$ is invertible, the **reduced physical form** is

$$\delta y_\ell=A_\ell\delta\chi+B_\ell h,
\qquad
A_\ell=S_\ell M_\ell^{-1}G_{\chi,\ell},\quad
B_\ell=H_{S,\ell}+S_\ell M_\ell^{-1}H_{D,\ell}.\tag{2.2}$$

There is no extra free $K_{\ell,r}c_\ell$ in the reduced form. A combined model

$$F_{\mathrm{aug}}(\chi,x,c)=F_{\mathrm{phys}}(\chi,x)+S(x)U_r(x)c\tag{2.3}$$

is legal only when explicitly declared as a model-error/current-correction model or a
conservative envelope (equivalently it adds $MU_rc$ to the state equation, so it is not the
original defect-free state equation). Fixed-dielectric multifrequency experiments must not
pretend that the same unscaled scattering potential at different frequencies is a valid
shared model.

### 0.4 Whitening / realification factor 2 (from §2.3)

For $\varepsilon\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,\Sigma)$, $\mathbb E[\varepsilon\varepsilon^*]=\Sigma$,
the real covariance of $\mathcal E(W\varepsilon)$ is $I/2$, not $I$. Two legal conventions:

$$K_{\mathbb R}=\sqrt2\,\mathcal R(WK),\quad
A_{\mathbb R}=\sqrt2\,\mathcal E(WA),\quad
B_{\mathbb R}=\sqrt2\,\mathcal E(WB),\tag{2.4}$$

so the real noise covariance is $I$; or keep the complex embedding and explicitly record
that **Fisher equals 2 × Gram**. The factor does not affect rank, principal angles, or
relative $\rho$, but it does affect absolute precision gates. Improper or cross-acquisition
correlated noise requires full real-covariance whitening; the resulting current block need
not stay per-frame block diagonal.

### 0.5 Joint quotient before map/pose blocking (from §2.4)

With a joint parameter metric $M_\theta\succ0$, take a complement basis $Z$ of the gauge
satisfying $Z^TM_\theta Z=I$, form $T=[A\ B]Z$, and split target/nuisance tangents with
right inverses $R$ and $N$ ($LR=I$ for a quotient-well-defined target $Lz$):

$$T_{\mathrm{tar}}=TR,\qquad T_{\mathrm{nui}}=TN.$$

$P_{\operatorname{Ran}(TN)^\perp}TR$ is invariant to the choice of right inverse, so
efficient information is right-inverse independent. **Absolute pose is not a target on an
unanchored quotient**: external anchors or explicit gauge fixing are required before the
clean independent $A,B$ blocks can be used. New unanchored acquisitions cannot remove the
global rigid-body gauge; source-model symmetry stabilizers are a separate phenomenon.

### 0.6 Pseudoinverse and projector definitions (from §2.5)

For $M_c\succ0,N_y\succ0$,

$$S^\dagger_{M_c,N_y}
=M_c^{-1/2}\left(N_y^{1/2}SM_c^{-1/2}\right)^\dagger N_y^{1/2},\tag{2.5}$$

$$P_U^{M_c}=U(U^*M_cU)^{-1}U^*M_c,\qquad
G=U^*M_cU,\quad T=N_y^{1/2}SUG^{-1/2},\tag{2.6}$$

$$C=G^{-1/2}T^\dagger N_y^{1/2}H_S,\qquad (SU)^*N_yR=0.\tag{2.7}$$

Only $H_S$ is lifted this way; do not lift total $B$ and then add $H_D$.

## E1. Matched coherent versus phaseless information

**Source lines.** Blueprint 2146–2158; supported by G0 243–366 and Theorem 1 368–535.

### E1 hypotheses and nulls (translated)

**Hypothesis.** In a *matched* experiment the joint information and the efficient (map or
pose target) information do not increase when coherent observations are replaced by their
phaseless transform; whether the efficient inequality is strict is governed by whether the
coherent efficient score is determined by the magnitude/intensity (measurability of the
efficient score with respect to the phaseless observation).

**Null / control family (translated list from §7 E1).**
1. Pure phase with known magnitude (remove phase, keep magnitude);
2. unknown nuisance phase;
3. circularly symmetric variance/scale family;
4. total field with a known reference;
5. deliberately mismatched independent intensity noise (an implementation/mismatch control,
   not evidence against data processing).

The blueprint mandates the first minimal reproduction to be the **four scalar controls**.
The blueprint failure criterion also requires equality controls: a strict joint gain together
with equal efficient information is an expected result, not a failure.

### E1 models (from §7 E1 plus Theorem 1 examples; exact formulas)

Scalar complex-Gaussian parent observations; the phaseless likelihood must be the exact
induced magnitude/intensity distribution, never a made-up homoskedastic Gaussian
“intensity” likelihood.

Theorem 1 setup: the statistical model is differentiable, score square-integrable,
differentiation through the observation channel is allowed, $Z$ is generated from $Y$ by a
parameter-independent Markov channel, and both experiments compare the same parameter,
nuisance family, and quotient target. Then

$$s_Z=\mathbb E[s_Y\mid Z],\qquad
J_Z\preceq J_Y,\qquad
J_{\mathrm{eff},Z}\preceq J_{\mathrm{eff},Y},\tag{3.3}$$

including singular nuisance Fisher. With $T$ the conditional-expectation operator,
$\mathcal H_Y$ the closed nuisance-score space, $\mathcal H_Z=\overline{T\mathcal H_Y}$,
$e_Y=(I-P_{\mathcal H_Y})s_v$, and $e_Z=(I-P_{\mathcal H_Z})Te_Y$, the exact identity used
for E1 metrics is

$$\|e_Y\|^2-\|e_Z\|^2
=\|e_Y-Te_Y\|^2+\|P_{\mathcal H_Z}Te_Y\|^2.\tag{3.4}$$

Equality holds iff the coherent efficient score is recoverable from $Z$. In finite dimension,

$$J_{\mathrm{eff}}=J_{\tau\tau}-J_{\tau\eta}J_{\eta\eta}^{\dagger}J_{\eta\tau}.$$

Concrete scalar examples (all are explicit E1 model/data formulas):

1. $Y=ae^{ix}+\varepsilon$, $\varepsilon\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,\sigma^2)$, $a>0$ known:
   the distribution of $|Y|$ is independent of $x$; coherent information is $2a^2/\sigma^2$.
2. $Y=ae^{i(x+\eta)}+\varepsilon$ with unknown nuisance phase $\eta$: efficient $x$
   information is zero in *both* experiments (strict joint inequality is not sufficient for
   strict target inequality).
3. Zero-mean circularly symmetric Gaussian scale family: the score depends only on $|Y|^2$,
   so magnitude retains all scale information (a positive equality example).
4. Fixed-covariance coherent Gaussian mean model:

$$J=2\operatorname{Re}(D\mu^*\Sigma^{-1}D\mu),$$

with a covariance-score term added whenever covariance depends on parameters (never use only
the mean-Jacobian Gram).
5. Raw-observation matching: if the incident receive field depends on unknown pose,
$|Y^{\mathrm{sca}}+e_{\mathrm{rx}}(x)|^2$ is **not** a parameter-independent channel derived
from the scattered field $Y^{\mathrm{sca}}$. Use the raw coherent total field as the common
parent experiment, or include the reference measurement together with noise. Example:

$$|c+ae^{ix}|^2=c^2+a^2+2ac\cos x,$$

which is phase-sensitive; a pure-phase tangent of the scattered field must not be treated
automatically as a null direction of total intensity.

### E1 metrics/plots

- Joint and efficient Fisher eigenvalues (map and pose targets);
- the two right-hand-side terms of Eq. (3.4): $\|e_Y-Te_Y\|^2$ and
  $\|P_{\mathcal H_Z}Te_Y\|^2$;
- score-conditioning error (residual of conditioning the coherent efficient score on $Z$);
- CRB versus local estimation variance.

### E1 budget and constants

- Seeds **101–110**;
- each model, each seed: **2,000 proposed/simulated samples**;
- minimal reproduction: four scalar controls first (analytic examples + likelihood/score
  checks).

### E1 success/failure criteria

**Failure:** violation of contraction (efficient/joint information increase) outside exact
model/integration error; or reported strictness conflicting with the equality controls
(e.g. strictness where efficient score is $Z$-measurable, or equality claimed where the
score is not recoverable).

## E2. Rank monotonicity, neutral admission, and rank uncertainty

**Source lines.** Blueprint 2160–2170; supported by Theorems 5–7, §3.9, Theorem 10, and
§4.2 gates (lines 939–1224, 1225–1380, 1644–1750, 1823–1881).

### E2 hypotheses and nulls (translated)

**Hypothesis.** Equations (3.17), (3.18), and (3.20) hold; $\rho$ need **not** be monotone
under rank expansion; and a relative-only threshold misjudges (false pass) at saturation.

**Controls (translated list).**
1. New directions orthogonal / partially aligned / fully aligned with the visible pose
   tangent (equality / strict-loss / complete-hiding cases);
2. complex-linear safe current spaces (real subspaces invariant under multiplication by
   $i$/$J_c$);
3. the three-dimensional nonmonotone example Eq. (3.19);
4. $N(t)=te_1$ rank uncertainty;
5. fixed-rank gap closure (coordinate-instability event).

### E2 model formulas (from §7 E2 and Theorems 5–6)

Fixed all physical tangents, metrics, whitening, gauge, frequency, and linearization.
Nested current spaces

$$C_r\subseteq C_{r+1},\qquad N_r=C_r+\operatorname{Ran}A,\qquad
E_r=N_{r+1}\cap N_r^\perp.$$

**Exact loss (hypothesis item 1):**

$$J_x(r)-J_x(r+1)=B^TP_{E_r}B\succeq0.\tag{3.17}$$

with

$$J_x(r)=J_x(r+1)\iff P_{E_r}B=0;$$
$$h^T[J_x(r)-J_x(r+1)]h>0\iff P_{E_r}Bh\ne0;$$
$$J_x(r)-J_x(r+1)\succ0\iff P_{E_r}B\text{ is injective on the pose chart}.$$

**Rank-drop identity (hypothesis item 2):**

$$\operatorname{rank}B_v(r)-\operatorname{rank}B_v(r+1)
=\dim\!\left(\operatorname{Ran}B_v(r)\cap E_r\right).\tag{3.18}$$

Supporting example showing information loss without rank drop: $B=e_1+e_2$ with an added
nuisance $e_1$ gives information $2\to1$ while rank stays 1. Also

$$K_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)=A^TP_{(C_r+\operatorname{Ran}B)^\perp}A$$

is Loewner-nonincreasing as the free-current space expands. Correct wording: additional free
current can improve representability or reduce model bias, but does not increase absolute
effective map/pose information in this fixed statistical experiment.

**Nonmonotone ρ example (three-dimensional, exact):**

$$A=(1,1,0)^T,\quad B=(1,0,1)^T,\quad
C_0=\{0\},\quad C_1=\operatorname{span}e_1,\quad
C_2=\operatorname{span}\{e_1,e_2+e_3\},$$

$$\rho(0)=3/4,\qquad \rho(1)=1,\qquad \rho(2)=0.\tag{3.19}$$

The first step removes a common map–pose component; the second makes the remaining map and
pose signatures collinear. $K_0$ can be kept nonzero at every step.

**Calibration-neutral admission (hypothesis item 3):** with existing nuisance $N$,
$V=P_{N^\perp}B$ the visible pose tangent, and candidate new current signatures
$Q=P_{N^\perp}K_+$,

$$V^TQZ=0\tag{3.20}$$

is necessary and sufficient for admitting $QZ$ without lowering existing pose information.
The largest safe coefficient space is $\ker(V^TQ)$, with dimension

$$d-\operatorname{rank}(V^TQ)\ge d-p.\tag{3.21}$$

For a genuinely complex-linear candidate space with $J_c=\begin{bmatrix}0&-I\\I&0\end{bmatrix}$,
$F=V^TQ$, the largest complex-linear safe space after realification is

$$\ker F\cap\ker(FJ_c),\tag{3.22}$$

which is $J_c$-invariant; safe complex dimension is at least $r-p$ when the candidate
push-forward is injective. Calibration-neutral does not imply sufficient expressivity: the
needed current may lie in the excluded directions, so the bias gate (E3) is mandatory.

**Rank uncertainty (supporting formula):** with $N(t)=te_1$, $B=e_1$,

$$J_x(0)=1,\qquad J_x(t)=0\quad(t\ne0).\tag{3.39}$$

Arbitrarily small matrix errors can change exact free-nuisance information. A verifiable
certificate requires structural constant rank (e.g. known $d$ columns with smallest column
singular value above the certified error bound), otherwise only a *numerically resolvable
rank* or a conditional diagnostic may be reported — never an exact-rank certificate.
Fixed-rank gap closure (projector jumping with retained rank fixed) is a
coordinate-instability event and is distinct from rank thresholds and observability
thresholds (§3.9.D).

### E2 model/data structure and budget

- Exact small matrix examples first (deterministic); then **12 fixed full-wave
  linearizations**, seeds **201–212**. All physics, whitening, metrics, and gauge are held
  fixed during each rank sweep; linearization points come from nonoracle iterates.
- No repeated nonlinear inversion is required for the linear theorem checks.

### E2 metrics/plots

- Loewner difference $J_x(r)-J_x(r+1)$ (predicted versus measured eigenvalues);
- predicted versus actual rank drop $\operatorname{rank}B_v(r)-\operatorname{rank}B_v(r+1)$;
- neutral-block loss (loss incurred by calibration-neutral admission directions);
- absolute backward-error rank threshold (not a fixed relative fraction of $\sigma_1$;
  threshold set by noise/error budget, gap must exceed operator uncertainty);
- projector distance (hard projector distance versus soft-filter distance; retained-boundary
  gap controls the bound
  $\|P(H+\Delta H)-P(H)\|\le 2\epsilon/(\delta-\epsilon)$ for $\|\Delta H\|\le\epsilon<\delta$).

### E2 success/failure criteria

**Success:** errors shrink with arithmetic precision or with certified error (i.e. the
implemented identities converge at the expected rate); nonlinear reconstruction outcomes
are **not** used to validate the linear theorems.

**Failure (implied by the success line and by Theorem 10):** the identity (3.17)/(3.18)
fails beyond its backward-error bound; a small relative ratio of roundoff-level singular
values is counted as physical rank; or a saturated nuisance codimension is declared
“zero projected rank” without recording the absolute numerical-rank tolerance.

## E3. Independent predictive role of the dual spectra and the bias gate

**Source lines.** Blueprint 2172–2182; supported by Theorem 3 (691–855), Theorem 4
(856–938), and Theorem 7 (1149–1224).

### E3 hypotheses and null (translated)

**Hypothesis.** $\rho$ predicts *relative* map degradation; the *absolute scale* of
$B_{\mathrm{vis}}$ predicts pose-noise amplification; **neither can substitute for the bias
bound**.

**Null.** After including absolute scale, residual, and bias information, the dual-spectrum
(bispectrum / $\rho$–$B_{\mathrm{vis}}$) quantities provide no additional predictive role.

**Controls (translated list).**
1. Fixed $\rho$ while scaling all signals (isolate absolute scale);
2. fixed pose rank while varying the minimum singular value;
3. strong (map/pose) priors;
4. small-residual / large-bias truncation cases.

### E3 model formulas

With $C=\operatorname{Ran}K$, $\Pi=P_{C^\perp}$, $A_c=\Pi A$, $B_c=\Pi B$:

$$K_0=A_c^TA_c,\qquad
K_{\mathrm{eff}}=A_c^T(I-P_{B_c})A_c,\qquad
J_x=B_c^T(I-P_{A_c})B_c.\tag{3.8}$$

For $a=\operatorname{rank}A_c$, $b=\operatorname{rank}B_c$ and orthonormal range bases
$Q_A,Q_B$, $C_{\mathrm{can}}=Q_A^TQ_B$, the two normalized Schur complements are congruent to

$$R_A=I_a-C_{\mathrm{can}}C_{\mathrm{can}}^T,\qquad
R_B=I_b-C_{\mathrm{can}}^TC_{\mathrm{can}}.\tag{3.9}$$

With $c_i=\cos\theta_i$, $i=1,\dots,\min(a,b)$, the map-side generalized spectrum is

$$\{\sin^2\theta_i\}_{i=1}^{\min(a,b)}\cup\{1\}^{a-\min(a,b)},\tag{3.10}$$

and the pose-side normalized spectrum is the same with $b-\min(a,b)$ extra ones.
Multiplicities:

$$\operatorname{mult}(\rho=0)=\dim(\operatorname{Ran}A_c\cap\operatorname{Ran}B_c),\qquad
\operatorname{mult}(\rho=1)=a-\operatorname{rank}C_{\mathrm{can}};$$

on $\ker K_0$ the pencil is $0/0$ and must not be assigned $\rho=0$ or $\rho=1$. Raw
$\sigma_i(B_{\mathrm{vis}})$ and $\rho_i$ are **not the same spectrum**: $\rho_i$ is
normalized by known-pose information and carries no absolute scale. Example: $B=0$ gives
$\rho=1$ on all visible map modes while pose has no information — the dual gate cannot
replace a pose-side check.

PSD pose prior extension:

$$K_{\mathrm{eff},\Lambda}=K_0-A_c^TB_c(B_c^TB_c+\Lambda_x)^\dagger B_c^TA_c,\tag{3.11}$$

$$K_{\mathrm{eff}}\preceq K_{\mathrm{eff},\Lambda}\preceq K_0,\qquad
v^TK_{\mathrm{eff},\Lambda}v=\min_h\{\|A_cv-B_ch\|^2+h^T\Lambda_xh\},\tag{3.12}$$

with augmented-space principal angles

$$A_{\mathrm{aug}}=\begin{bmatrix}A_c\\0\end{bmatrix},\qquad
B_{\mathrm{aug}}=\begin{bmatrix}B_c\\\Lambda_x^{1/2}\end{bmatrix}.\tag{3.13}$$

Map priors are analogous; a cross-block prior must be augmented jointly; deterministic
regularization curvature is not “new data Fisher.”

**Bias-aware risk (the metric used for the bias gate):** in the fixed linear experiment
$y=Na+Bh+D_rz+\varepsilon$, $\|z\|\le1$, $\varepsilon\sim N(0,I)$, with $D_rz$ the declared
truncation/model-error term, $B_v=P_{N^\perp}B$ full column rank, and
$\widehat h=B_v^\dagger y$:

$$\sup_{\|z\|\le1}\mathbb E\|\widehat h-h\|_{M_x}^2
=\operatorname{tr}(M_xJ_x^{-1})+\|M_x^{1/2}B_v^\dagger D_r\|_2^2.\tag{3.23}$$

Four old relative gates ($\eta_S,g_S,\eta_x,\eta_\rho$) can all look good while
$\|B_v^\dagger D_r\|$ is large; the required Pareto items are: representation-error bound
$\beta_r$, absolute pose/map precision, and computation cost. $D_r$ must come from a declared
model set / uniform bound / certified numerical error — never post hoc from test truth. If
rank is selected adaptively on the same data, covariance (3.23) is not the exact post-selection
covariance; use bounds valid simultaneously for all admissible ranks, or independent
selection/validation.

The map-side relative retention definition used by the gates:

$$\eta_{x,\mathrm{all}}=\inf_{h:\,Bh\ne0}\frac{h^TJ_xh}{h^TB^TBh},$$

which measures the relative loss from “current/map known” to “unknown”; it is distinct from
the Theorem-3 cleaned canonical normalization.

### E3 metrics and budget

- Linear Gaussian covariance (empirical versus fixed-tangent inverse information);
- Theorem-7 risk formula (3.23): variance trace plus squared worst-case bias;
- target coverage (including adaptive-rank selection consequences);
- relative versus absolute error (relative-only gate failures at high bias/saturation).

**Budget:** seeds **301–310**; **1,000 proposed noise repetitions per fixed design**;
nonlinear small-perturbation tests are a separate extrapolation and must not be mixed with
the linear-theorem results.

### E3 success/failure criteria

The §7 E3 block gives hypotheses, null, controls, metrics, and budget but **no explicit
numeric success/failure line**; the intended falsification is that relative-only gates pass a
high-bias case (the relative-only policy is then falsified as intended) and that the dual
spectra add nothing after absolute scale/residual/bias are included. The English protocol’s
E3 adds the operative acceptance rule: fixed-linear estimator must match analytic covariance
and bias within Monte Carlo uncertainty; nonlinear predictive superiority requires a
predeclared held-out comparison. Treat this as an implementation decision to be locked with
the parent Codex thread.

## E5. Shared-map stacking, symmetry, and acquisition budget

**Source lines.** Blueprint 2292–2304; supported by Theorem 8 (1381–1597) and Theorem 9
(1598–1643).

### E5 hypothesis and nulls (translated)

**Hypothesis.** New information comes from the *inconsistency of shared-map compensation*
across acquisitions, not from the frame count itself.

**Controls (translated list).**
1. $a_1=a_2=1$, $b_1=-b_2$ (minimal two-frame contrast);
2. repeated/duplicate acquisition;
3. new frame-specific nuisance that fills the new data (saturation: cleaned $a,b$ become
   zero);
4. unanchored global gauge;
5. single isotropic source stabilizer versus directional/multiple sources.

### E5 model formulas

After eliminating acquisition-specific currents, cleaned per-frame tangents are
$a_\ell,b_\ell$. The stacked pose direction $h$ is hidden iff one common map direction $u$
compensates every frame:

$$a_\ell u=b_\ell h\qquad\forall\ell.\tag{3.28}$$

Equivalently the frame compensation sets $\mathcal U_\ell(h)=a_\ell^\dagger b_\ell h+\ker a_\ell$
have a common intersection. If each $a_\ell$ is full column rank and every per-frame pose is
hidden, with $T_\ell=a_\ell^\dagger b_\ell$, the stacked pose is identifiable iff

$$\begin{bmatrix}T_2-T_1\\ \vdots\\ T_L-T_1\end{bmatrix}
\text{ is full column rank}.\tag{3.29}$$

The required acquisition count bound is then

$$(L-1)q\ge p,\qquad L\ge1+\lceil p/q\rceil.\tag{3.30}$$

(Reachable for generic algebraic matrix families but must not be claimed as a
Helmholtz/Maxwell sampling genericity theorem without proof.)

**Why per-frame elimination then summation is wrong:**

$$h^TJ_{\mathrm{stack}}h=\min_u\sum_\ell\|a_\ell u-b_\ell h\|^2
\ge\sum_\ell\min_{u_\ell}\|a_\ell u_\ell-b_\ell h\|^2,$$

$$J_{\mathrm{stack}}\succeq\sum_\ell J_\ell,\tag{3.31}$$

with equality iff the per-frame optimal compensations share one common choice for every $h$.
Minimal example:

$$a_1=a_2=1,\qquad b_1=1,\ b_2=-1,$$

each per-frame $J_\ell=0$, but

$$J_{\mathrm{stack}}=\min_u[(u-h)^2+(u+h)^2]/h^2=2.$$

Duplicating $b_2=b_1$ gives zero stacked gain. If a new frame’s free current already fills
its data space, cleaned $a,b$ are both zero and that frame adds no joint-map information.

**Acquisition innovation formula.** With old cleaned blocks $A,B$ and
$G=A^TA\succ0$ on the full declared shared-map nuisance chart,
$H=G^{-1}A^TB$, $J=B^T(I-P_A)B$, and new cleaned blocks $a,b$:

$$J_{\mathrm{new}}=J+V^T(I+aG^{-1}a^T)^{-1}V,\qquad V=b-aH.\tag{3.32}$$

Consequences:

$$J_{\mathrm{new}}=J\iff V=0,\qquad \ker J_{\mathrm{new}}=\ker J\cap\ker V.\tag{3.33}$$

If $G$ is singular, revert to the variational formula (do not silently restrict the map to the
old observable support); a legal fixed map prior may be appended as prior rows to make $G$
invertible.

**Design gradient / guarantee boundary:**

$$dJ=2\,\operatorname{sym}\{R^T(dB-dA\,H)\},\qquad R=B-AH,\ A^TR=0.\tag{3.34}$$

The pose-Schur $\log\det(J+\Lambda_x)$ is generally **not submodular**: the two-frame example
has zero per-frame gain and positive joint gain, directly violating diminishing returns, and
small positive map priors can preserve the violation. Greedy/pair-look-ahead is permitted;
the $1-1/e$ approximation guarantee is not transferable.

**Rank–acquisition budget (Theorem 9).** Enlarging free current from $r$ to $r+1$ on the old
acquisitions costs

$$\mathcal L_{\mathrm{rank}}=B^TP_{E_r}B,$$

then an acquisition evaluated after that enlargement contributes

$$\mathcal I_{\mathrm{acq}}=V^T(I+aG^{-1}a^T)^{-1}V,$$

and

$$J_{\mathrm{final}}-J_{\mathrm{original}}=\mathcal I_{\mathrm{acq}}-\mathcal L_{\mathrm{rank}}.\tag{3.35}$$

Therefore

$$J_{\mathrm{final}}\succeq J_{\mathrm{original}}
\iff\mathcal I_{\mathrm{acq}}\succeq\mathcal L_{\mathrm{rank}}.\tag{3.36}$$

This is the necessary-and-sufficient *local* “expand rank, then add acquisition without
lowering pose information” budget, not a global basin theorem.

### E5 model/data structure and budget

- Small-matrix exact validation, then **8 physical acquisition candidates**;
- seeds **401–412**;
- compare: single greedy, pair look-ahead, random selection, and exhaustive selection over
  the small candidate set (matrix scoring exhaustive is allowed for 8 candidates; it is
  charged if it requires new forward/adjoint computations).

### E5 metrics/plots

- Difference between the exact shared-map Schur result and the wrong per-frame-summed result
  ($J_{\mathrm{stack}}$ versus $\sum_\ell J_\ell$);
- hidden dimension (rank of the hiding condition and $\dim\ker J_{\mathrm{new}}$);
- innovation rank (rank of $V$ and of the innovation term);
- predicted $\mathcal I_{\mathrm{acq}}-\mathcal L_{\mathrm{rank}}$ versus the actual matrix
  difference $J_{\mathrm{final}}-J_{\mathrm{original}}$.

### E5 failure criteria (translated verbatim)

1. Writing an increase in replicated-data precision as a rank increase;
2. writing the disappearance of a source stabilizer as disappearance of the global gauge;
3. actual gain failing to satisfy the declared safe budget
   ($\mathcal I_{\mathrm{acq}}\succeq\mathcal L_{\mathrm{rank}}$).
