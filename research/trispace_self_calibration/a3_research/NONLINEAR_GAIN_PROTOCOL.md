# Nonlinear gain-graph mechanism check (development)

Registered 2026-09-08 before data evaluation. Addresses the missing bridge
between unrestricted-gain graph theory and experiments with known electronics.
This is NOT a final acquisition-policy study or a new identifiability theorem.

Use a multiple-scattering two-sphere Treams field at k=9, six receiver locations
and four transverse illuminations. Observe one fixed linear receive
polarization per location. A finite bank explicitly contains the true physical
parameters plus four fixed wrong pose/material alternatives. The true candidate
is an ORACLE positive control for conditional coverage, not a generated-bank
success. Repeat two prescribed noise/gain seeds 8301–8302 at 30 dB.

Compare two fixed nine-edge patterns. A spanning tree covers all six receivers
and four illuminations. A cycle-rich graph uses three receivers and four
illuminations, with three complex cycles. Equal edge count does not mean equal
angular coverage or equal active-nuisance dimension: those differences are the
declared graph mechanism. Do not call this an optimized policy advantage.

For each candidate, fit unrestricted separable complex receiver/transmitter
gains to the same observed edges, anchor one transmitter to remove scaling
gauge, and use three common deterministic starts. Analytic gain derivatives
must pass finite differences. A spanning-tree constructive fit must reproduce
arbitrary nonzero candidate-to-data ratios to roundoff. All starts and statuses
are retained, including failures. Scores are profiled training residuals, NOT
independent-validation risk certificates. Record best-candidate ranking with
and without the true candidate, wrong-candidate score minima, all runtime and
graph cycle counts. Shared exact reference/inverse physics is intentional for
this mechanism test and must not be described as independent-model inversion.
